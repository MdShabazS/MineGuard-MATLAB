## 1. Summary

**One seeded, triangulated world model is the only geometry source.**
- The terrain is a 161×161 grid at 1 m spacing: 25,921 vertices and 51,200 triangles over 160×160 m.
- Its relief comes from a gentle slope, seeded Fourier modes, a mound and a shallow gully. The home pad is blended flat.
- The surface colour is a soil, grass and dry-patch albedo driven by noise and slope.
- **Objects:** 45 rocks, 30 bushes and 4 survey-corner markers, each **seated on its own mesh footprint** (no vertex floats).
- 2 painted calibration landmarks.
- `heightAt` and `albedoAt` interpolate on **exactly the drawn triangles**, so a height query and the rendered ground agree to 1e-9 m (test s04).

**The aircraft** is a physically scaled F450-class quadrotor in the FLU body frame:
- 0.45 m motor-to-motor diagonal and 0.254 m propeller discs
- red/orange front arms and grey rear arms, so heading is readable
- skid landing gear and a nadir payload pod

The body origin sits at the gear-contact plane, so a pose at ground height stands the aircraft on the ground and never in it (s03, s08).

**The renderer** (`mgv2.scene.SceneRenderer`) uses base MATLAB graphics:
- static geometry is drawn once;
- each frame updates one `hgtransform`, the ground shadow, the altitude stalk, a capped trail, the camera and the HUD;
- a sun light plus a fill light, a sky background, a draped survey boundary, the home pad marking and a north arrow;
- **SIMULATION** HUD and banner.

**It never owns the clock**: `update(pose)` only displays the PoseSample it is given.

**Five operator cameras** come from a pure, tested function (`mgv2.scene.cameraView`):
- **isometric:** whole field.
- **follow:** world-fixed offset, centred on the aircraft.
- **chase:** behind along the heading, with the heading smoothed in simulation time.
- **top-down:** survey area, north up.
- **orbit:** close inspection with configurable azimuth, elevation and distance.

Follow, chase and orbit keep the aircraft at the view centre, so it cannot be lost in turns, climbs or landing (s06, s09, s10).

**Sensor geometry for V2-005** (`mgv2.scene.sensorRig`, PROVISIONAL pending V2-004):
- nadir RGB 640×360 with 70° HFOV, and nadir thermal 160×120 with 50° HFOV, using pinhole intrinsics and explicit body→sensor extrinsics;
- a nadir LiDAR entry;
- functions: `sensorPose`, `projectToImage`, `pixelRays`, `intersectTerrain`, `cameraFootprint`, and a scene-sampled raster `renderNadirRaster` for feasibility;
- `toUavScenario` exports **the same triangles** as UAV Toolbox `"custom"` meshes. The toolbox LiDAR returns ranges matching `heightAt` (s17).

Research, the measured renderer spike and the decisions are in [`v2/docs/V2_002_003_ENGINEERING_DECISIONS.md`](../docs/V2_002_003_ENGINEERING_DECISIONS.md), committed with V2-002.

## 2. Interfaces and frames

- **World:** ENU metres (x east, y north, z up above the virtual datum), `schema_version "mg-v2-scene/0.1"` (provisional, V2-003 owned). There are no target, truth or label fields, and the config is an exact allowlist (s18).
- **Engine coupling:**
  - `mgv2.scene.terrainProvider(world)` gives the V2-002 engine a `{id, heightAt}` provider, so it flies over the drawn ground.
  - The renderer consumes V2-002 PoseSamples (`position_enu_m`, `orientation_quat` [w x y z] FLU→ENU, `t_s`, `yaw_rad`, optional `phase`, `altitude_agl_m`) and validates them (`mgv2:InvalidPose`, s19).
- **Camera frame:**
  - x right, y down (image), z along the optical axis.
  - Nadir mounting: optical axis = body −z, image up = aircraft forward (s11).
  - Pixel coordinates are 1-based with pixel centres at integers.
- **UAV Toolbox LiDAR convention (verified):** on a NED/FRD `uavPlatform`, the sensor frame follows FRD, so **positive elevation points down**. `ElevationLimits [60 90]` sees the ground and `[-90 -60]` does not. The sensor must also be mounted outside the platform's own body mesh, or it returns self-hits (found and fixed during testing, s17).

### Defects found by testing and fixed before the clean run
1. **Floating objects:** objects were seated on a 12-point ring, but jittered meshes extend beyond it, giving up to 17 mm of float. They are now seated on the actual mesh footprint with a 3 cm embed.
2. **Handle-name collision:** the UAV screen marker overwrote the survey-corner marker group handle, so cleanup left one object behind. Both are now renamed.
3. **LiDAR self-hit:** the sensor returned 5 mm hits from the platform's own body. It is now mounted outside the body, and the FRD elevation sign is documented.
4. **HUD:** the TeX interpreter turned `RETURN_HOME` into a subscript. Now escaped, with a regression check.
5. **Visual review of the first render:** the terrain was too dark and the axes box walls were visible. The albedo and lighting were rebalanced, the axes hidden on a sky background, and a north arrow added.

## 8. Known limitations and outstanding issues

1. **macOS: NOT TESTED.** Rendering speed and appearance on Apple-silicon graphics are unknown. On the user's Mac, from a clean checkout:
   ```matlab
   cd <repo>; addpath('v2/matlab');
   r = runtests({'v2/matlab/tests/testV2002MissionEngine.m','v2/matlab/tests/testV2003Scene.m'}); table(r)
   mgv2SceneDemo('RealTime', 1)       % report the printed frame median/p95 and sim/wall ratio
   mgv2CameraGeometryDemo
   ```
2. **Rendering is native MATLAB graphics.** It is credible engineering visualisation, not photorealism: there are no cast shadows except the aircraft's ground shadow, no textures beyond per-vertex colour, and no Unreal (unsupported on macOS).
3. **The camera rasters are a feasibility proof only.** They are terrain-only (rocks, bushes and markers are not ray-traced), with Lambert shading and no noise, exposure or distortion. The final RGB/thermal/LiDAR streams belong to V2-005 after V2-004 freezes the sensor contract. The rig values are **PROVISIONAL**.
4. Rotors are static discs (no spin animation), chosen to keep one transform per frame.
5. **Performance:** the measured frame time varies between runs on the development host. Live demo at 1× in the clean clone: median 6.7 ms, p95 66.0 ms, max 211.8 ms (the max includes screenshot frames), 9.9 FPS effective at the decimated 10 Hz target. An earlier working-copy live run gave a 31.0 ms median. In the same suite a pre-fix run gave a 53–55 ms median, while the post-fix runs gave 23–26 ms. Both are within the ≥ 10 FPS target, and the scene is decimated to every 2nd engine tick (10 Hz). It must be re-measured on the Mac (AC-025, V2-008).
6. `intersectTerrain` marches in 0.25 m steps. Terrain features thinner than that could be stepped over (not present in this smooth world).

## 9. Research

Research: [`v2/docs/V2_002_003_ENGINEERING_DECISIONS.md`](../docs/V2_002_003_ENGINEERING_DECISIONS.md) records the verified R2026b API facts. They cover `show3D` FastUpdate, `addMesh` mesh types and the ENU default, `terrainHeight` failing on surface meshes, `uavLidarPointCloudGenerator` on scenario meshes, `uavPlatform.move`, and Unreal being unsupported on macOS. It also records the measured spike (`show3D` 67.4 ms vs `hgtransform` 3.9 ms per frame), the decisions D4–D6 and the Crossref-verified literature. The pinhole projection, ray back-projection and footprint follow the standard central-projection camera model, verified here by a closed-loop landmark test rather than by citation.
